HOW TO INTERPRET RETURNS

There are two return types commonly used to report monthly asset performance:
Monthly return
A monthly return measures the relative (%) change in an asset's price from the beginning of the current month to the end of the current month. E.g., a monthly return of 10% for an asset with a price of £110 at the beginning of the current month implies a price of £121 (= £110 × (1 + 10%)) at the end of the current month.
Return since purchase
A return since purchase measures the relative (%) change in an asset's price from the beginning of Month 1 (time of purchase) to the end of the current month. E.g., a return since purchase of 21% for an asset with a price of £100 at the beginning of Month 1 implies a price of £121 (= £100 × (1 + 21%)) at the end of the current month.
